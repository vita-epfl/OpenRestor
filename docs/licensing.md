# OpenRestor Licensing Guide

This document separates the licence of the OpenRestor software from the rights in the
audio, metadata, model weights and third-party assets. A software licence never relicenses
audio bundled with, processed by or indexed by the software.

This is a technical release policy, not legal advice. EPFL's Technology Transfer Office
(`TTO`) and the head of the laboratory remain the approval authorities for software
developed at EPFL.

For a source-by-source release decision, use the two dated inventories:
[academic non-commercial redistribution](redistribution_inventory_academic.md) and
[academic distribution with commercially compatible media](redistribution_inventory_commercial_ready.md).
Both distinguish a licence-compatible candidate from a file approved for upload.

The executable gate is the **private** `build/rights/media_registry.jsonl` generated by
`scripts/prepare_rights_profiles.sh`. For the current multi-dataset Clean audit, invoke
it with `CLEAN_CORPUS_MANIFEST=build/clean_corpus/rights_registry_input.jsonl`; otherwise
the wrapper uses the historical benchmark manifests. The wrapper does not mutate the
registry when called with `--help`. The registry stores licence label and exact URL (or `null`
where no licence URL is known), attribution, dated evidence, review status and nullable
`allow_commercial`, `allow_redistribution`, `allow_derivatives` fields for each audio
`source_id` or RIR file. `null` never grants a permission. The public candidate exposes
only aggregate counts. Software licensing and upload approval are separate decisions.

Pour enregistrer de nouveaux candidats multi-datasets sans accorder de permission :

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_media_rights_registry.py \
  --clean-corpus-manifest build/clean_corpus/curation_manifest.jsonl \
  --out build/rights/media_registry.jsonl \
  --summary build/rights/public_summary.json
```

Les lignes sont importées avec le statut existant (généralement `pending`) et les
champs de permission `null` lorsqu'ils ne sont pas prouvés. Une approbation nécessite
toujours un override privé avec licence exacte, attribution, preuve datée et reviewer.

## Creative Commons Terms In This Project

| Mark | Meaning | Commercial use | Adaptation | Consequence for OpenRestor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `CC0` | Public-domain dedication | yes | yes | May be published; attribution is good provenance practice but is not a licence condition. |
| `BY` | Attribution | yes | yes | May be published if the creator, source, licence link and modifications are credited. |
| `SA` | ShareAlike | yes unless combined with `NC` | yes | Adapted material must be distributed under the same or a compatible licence. OpenRestor v0.1 excludes it to avoid mixing obligations. |
| `NC` | NonCommercial | no | yes unless combined with `ND` | A possible candidate for an explicitly non-commercial academic pack after per-item review; excluded from a commercially compatible release. Current v0.1 audio remains withheld. |
| `ND` | NoDerivatives | yes unless combined with `NC` | **no** | Excluded: excerpting, loudness normalization and degradation are treated conservatively as adaptations. |

There is no standard Creative Commons `NS` condition. It is usually a confusion with `NC`,
`ND` or `SA`. Combined licences accumulate their conditions: for example `CC BY-NC-SA`
requires attribution, forbids commercial use and imposes ShareAlike.

The licence version also matters. A release notice must retain the exact version and URL,
for example `CC BY 3.0` rather than merely `CC BY`.

## Proposed Software And Metadata Terms

The current proposal is the **EPFL Source Code Academic License** for code and manifests
whose relevant rights EPFL owns. Academic terms would not grant commercial use of those
EPFL-controlled components; commercial users would need a separate EPFL licence. This is a
proposal, not a licence already granted by this repository. The TTO must confirm whether its
software template also covers manifests and any applicable database rights, or prepare a
separate data addendum. An academic restriction on OpenRestor metadata cannot relicense
underlying recordings or third-party source data.

SonicMaster's repository is Apache-2.0. `openrestor/degradations/ariel_effects.py` openly
describes itself as an adaptation of its degradation code, including a literal compressor
port. Preserve the original Apache-2.0 grant, copyright notices and a change notice for
that material. Merely placing an academic licence at the repository root would not cancel
those upstream permissions. The TTO should review the precise adapted-code boundary before
release. See `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

Before adding a project licence, EPFL's published process requires:

1. identify every contributor and whether they were an EPFL employee when contributing;
2. resolve assignments for code written by students, visitors or other non-employees;
3. check project/funding agreements and patent constraints;
4. obtain approval from the head of the laboratory; and
5. contact the TTO when ownership is mixed or uncertain.

After approval, add the TTO-approved academic licence and mark its exact scope; preserve
the complete Apache-2.0 text and required attribution for SonicMaster-derived code.
`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` records source-specific material and does not replace the upstream
licence. Complete `docs/templates/software_release_inventory.md` and use
`docs/templates/academic_supervisor_approval_request.md` to request a decision.

## SonicMaster/Jamendo Audit

The frozen split contains 25,000 distinct Jamendo `source_id` values. The audit run on
2026-10-16 resolved 16,144 through Jamendo's current API: 856 CC BY, 1,623 BY-SA, 13,638
with NC and/or ND, 23 without a licence URL, and 4 under Licence Art Libre. Another 8,856
IDs were not returned. See the complete decision record in
`docs/reports/sonicmaster_jamendo_licence_audit_2026-10-16.md`.

The 2026-10-16 audit is retained as historical evidence. **Do not use a new bulk API
acquisition as a shortcut to publication**: Jamendo's [API terms](https://devportal.jamendo.com/api_terms_of_use)
need review for commercial/offline access, and an API licence field is not independent
proof of the artist's authority or historical terms. If Jamendo approves a scoped
follow-up, the existing audit can technically be resumed with:

```bash
export JAMENDO_CLIENT_ID='<client id from https://devportal.jamendo.com/>'
scripts/audit_sonicmaster_jamendo_licenses.sh
```

Outputs are written below `build/licence-audits/sonicmaster_jamendo/`:

- `tracks.jsonl`: exact licence URL, attribution metadata and publication decision per ID;
- `summary.json` and `audit_report.md`: counts by licence and policy decision;
- `missing_source_ids.txt`: IDs not returned by Jamendo;
- `raw/`: original API responses retained as evidence.

The audit fails if the API fails or any source ID is missing. Its `BY` and `CC0` entries
are **unapproved candidates**, not redistributable audio. `NC`, `ND`, missing and unknown
licences are withheld. `SA` requires a separate pack and notice after source review.

## Requests For Written Confirmation

Send the SonicMaster question first as a public issue on
<https://github.com/AMAAI-Lab/SonicMaster/issues>, tagging the maintainers if appropriate.
The public record makes the answer auditable. The Jamendo question can be sent through the
official Jamendo Licensing support form at <https://support-licensing.jamendo.com/contact>,
with a copy to `support@jamendo.com`, the custom-licensing address published in Jamendo's
current terms.

Suggested message to SonicMaster/AMAAI:

> Subject: Per-track redistribution rights for SonicMaster clean Jamendo audio
>
> We are preparing OpenRestor, a public benchmark that would redistribute 30-second excerpts
> of the SonicMaster clean targets and new degraded derivatives. The Hugging Face dataset card
> labels SonicMaster CC BY 2.0, while the project page says the source recordings were obtained
> from Jamendo under Creative Commons licences. Our 16 October 2026 audit of all 25,000
> `source_id` values through Jamendo's official API returned only 856 CC BY tracks; 1,623 were
> BY-SA, 13,638 had NC and/or ND restrictions, 27 had missing or other licence records, and
> 8,856 were not returned. Could you explain this discrepancy and confirm whether AMAAI obtained
> separate authority to offer the **underlying recordings** under CC BY 2.0, rather than only
> the dataset packaging or metadata? If rights remain per track, could you provide the licence
> URL and required creator attribution keyed by `source_id`, preferably including historical
> evidence from the time of collection? We specifically need permission for commercial
> redistribution of 30-second excerpts and degraded adaptations.

Suggested message to Jamendo:

> Subject: Licence evidence for 25,000 Jamendo track IDs used in a research benchmark
>
> We are auditing 25,000 Jamendo track IDs contained in the public SonicMaster Dataset before
> redistributing 30-second excerpts and audio derivatives in OpenRestor. On 16 October 2026,
> the official `/v3.0/tracks` API with `include=licenses` resolved 16,144 IDs: only 856 were
> CC BY, while 13,638 had NC and/or ND restrictions, 1,623 were BY-SA, and 27 had missing or
> other licence records. The remaining 8,856 IDs were not returned. Can you confirm that
> `license_ccurl` is the governing licence for the underlying recording, explain what a missing
> API result means, and tell us whether historical licence records from the time SonicMaster
> was collected can be retrieved? Please also confirm the attribution fields that must be
> retained and whether Jamendo terms add restrictions to bulk public redistribution beyond
> each track's Creative Commons licence.
